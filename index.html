Who it's for

Anyone who gets asked "can I come and have a look?" more often than they have time for.

Estate and letting agents

Buyers walk the house before they book a viewing. The ones who do turn up have already seen the box room and still want it.

Schools and nurseries

Open evening happens once a year. A tour is there every day for the parents who couldn't come, and for the child who wants to see their classroom before September.

Wedding and event venues

Couples plan at eleven at night. Let them stand in the middle of the room and work out where the top table goes.

Hotels and holiday lets

Show every bedroom and the view from the window. It answers the "what's the second bedroom like?" email before anyone sends it.

Museums and galleries

Pin a note beside each exhibit. People can visit from the other side of the country, or come back for a second look after the school trip.

Showrooms and studios

Car showrooms, gyms, salons, offices to let. If people need to see the space before they commit, show them.

What goes into a tour

Notes where people look

An info point opens a short note on the spot: how old the staircase is, how many the hall seats, what's behind the locked door.

Signposts between rooms

Drop a hotspot on a doorway and visitors click through to the next room, the way they'd walk it.

Photo Video

Photos and video in one tour

Mix 360° stills and footage. A quick photo of the utility room can sit next to a walkthrough of the garden.

Your icons, your colours

Forty icons, from doors and stairs to books, coffee and the garden. Set the pin colours to match your brand, with a hex code if you have one.

On your website in one paste

Copy the embed code into a page, or host the exported folder yourself. A tour is a plain web page, so visitors don't install anything.

Your files stay with you

The editor runs in your browser. Photos and video stay on your computer until you choose to publish, and the tour you export is yours to host wherever you like.

How it works

  1. Shoot

    Take a 360° photo or video in each room. Any camera that saves equirectangular files will do. Insta360, Ricoh Theta and GoPro Max cameras all can.

  2. Link

    Upload them to the editor and each one becomes a scene. Add hotspots to join the scenes up, and info points for anything worth explaining.

  3. Share

    Export the tour and upload the folder to your web host, or paste the embed code into a page you already have.

Questions people ask

Do I need a special camera?

You need 360° photos or video, so a 360 camera or a phone app that stitches full panoramas. Showround takes equirectangular JPG and PNG photos, and MP4, WebM and MOV video.

Does it cost anything?

Not at the moment, and there's no account to create.

Where do my photos go?

Nowhere. The editor runs in your browser, so your files stay on your computer. When you export, you get a folder to upload to your own web host.

Can I change a tour after I've made it?

Yes. Keep the zip you exported, then use Import in the editor to open it again and carry on.

Do visitors need an app?

No. A finished tour is a web page, so it opens in a browser like any other page on your site.

Can I build a tour on my phone?

The editor needs a laptop or desktop screen for now.
